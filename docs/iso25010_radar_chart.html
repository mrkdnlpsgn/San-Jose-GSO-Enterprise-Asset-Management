A. The Spike

For this local e-government web-based inventory and tracking management system, Security and Reliability are prioritized at the highest level because the system handles sensitive ICT equipment records, accountable person information, and government asset data that must remain accurate and protected at all times. Functional Suitability is also highly emphasized to ensure the system correctly manages inventory records, tracks equipment movement through QR codes, and generates accurate reports for COA (Commission on Audit) compliance. Performance Efficiency and Usability remain important to allow quick and smooth processing of inventory operations at the municipal hall, though they are slightly less critical than data protection and accuracy. Compatibility is given moderate priority since the system integrates with external services such as PhilSMS for notifications and Gmail SMTP for email alerts. Other factors like Maintainability and Portability are given moderate priority since the system is primarily used in a fixed local government setting at the San Jose Municipal Hall ICT Division.

B. The Narrative

The system prioritizes Security and Reliability because it handles sensitive government ICT equipment records, accountable personnel details (including contact numbers and emails), and official inventory data where data integrity and protection are critical. The system enforces JWT-based authentication, BCrypt password hashing, role-based access control (Admin and Staff roles), rate limiting on login attempts, account lockout policies, and comprehensive audit logging of all operations. Functional Suitability is also high to ensure the inventory management, QR-based equipment tracking, device record linking, and SMS/email notification features accurately reflect equipment details and movement history during inventory audits and day-to-day operations. Other factors like Usability and Performance Efficiency remain important but slightly lower since the system mainly supports internal ICT asset management within a single municipal hall rather than public-facing digital services or high-traffic remote access scenarios.
